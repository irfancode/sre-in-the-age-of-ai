# Chapter 25 — Observing AI: OTel, Traces and the Economy of Tokens

Here's the 2026 observability confession: **most teams can tell you the CPU of the box the model runs in, and cannot tell you what the model was *thinking* when it cost them $4,000 in one night.** That's the gap this chapter plugs. You already own the discipline (Chapter 7); now you're upgrading the sensors.

## The one platform decision: OpenTelemetry, end of story

The industry converged. **OpenTelemetry (OTel)** is the neutral, vendor-agnostic standard for emitting your telemetry logs/metrics/traces, and its **GenAI semantic conventions** (in active standardization since 2024) define exactly how to trace *AI things*: LLM calls, agent invocations, tool executions, token counts, session-level metrics, finish-reasons.

Why you should care as a beginner:
- **Portability** — your AI observability is not owned by one vendor; you're not repo-confessional'd into a tool you'll someday ragequit.
- **The agents speak the same language** — whether your SRE agent, your orchestrators, or your *product's* agents, they all see the same standardized telemetry. An AI first-responder that reads your AI spans will correlate an agent failure *as cleanly* as it correlates a DB blowup.
- **The majors all speak it now** — Datadog, Honeycomb, New Relic; frameworks LangChain, CrewAI, AutoGen, AG2 emit compliant spans directly. Your job: make sure the stack you choose *emits* OTel GenAI spans. If a demo doesn't, it's cheating.

**The OpenTelemetry-first habit (the "instrument first" law):** before any optimization, before any dashboard, before *any* agent is trusted with anything — instrument. If you're flying without AI-inclusive telemetry at 3 a.m., you're a blindfolded pilot, and the co-pilot (your AI SRE) is a blindfolded pilot, too.

## The sensors that matter for AI systems (beyond the golden four)

The golden signals still apply; your *added* sensors are:

### The token economy (the new "saturation")
Tokens are to LLM systems what QPS is to a web service — **the load unit** — and they're entangled with *money*:
- **Tokens per task per agent** — the reliability + cost gauge. A slow upward creep is a textbook *leading* indicator: usually surfaces **24–48 hours before** visible output degradation. (Bloat → slower → pricier → dumber → loop.)
- **Token budget per run** — the hard guardrail that catches runaway loops; implement as a real deadline, not a wish.
- **Cost-per-request dashboards** — finance will love you; reliability will understand you; the "your agent spent $12k last night" call will *never* happen again (this is the incident nobody writes a postmortem for and everyone remembers).

### Context window fill
The context window is the agent's working memory; its fill ratio is **your saturation metric for the brain**. High fill → hallucinations rise, tools get dropped, tasks degrade. Two sensors and two verbs: *watch the ratio*; when it climbs, *compact, summarize, or start fresh*. (Chapter 19 told you memory is state — this is the gauge on that tank.)

### The `finish_reason` signal
A simple, underrated gold: the model's reported finish reason. A bloom in **`max_tokens`** (output chopped at a hard ceiling) is a *direct* signal of a context-management problem — truncated, malformed results masquerading as answers. Alert on max-tokens rates like you'd alert on OOM kill counts.

### The behavioral layer (judgment telemetry)
All of Chapter 24's meters — overrides, policy-compliance verdicts, tool-call outcomes, loop counts — flow through the same pipeline. **Judgment is telemetry; wire it like telemetry.** Politically (and this is the trick for your org): put a "behavior" panel next to "health" on *every* agent dashboard, so "alive" and "behaving" stop being conflated in the same glance.

### The model-version & provider observability
- **Model/prompt version pinned and visible on every trace** — so "which brain answered this user, and which brain answered it last Tuesday?" is a two-second query, not an archaeology project.
- **Provider latency + error SLIs** (upstream API): if 5% of your calls hit a 429/throttle wall, you have an availability-behavior story that belongs on your board.
- **Embeddings & retriever freshness** (Chapter 26): retrieval-recall alarms are your hallucination early-warning.

## The 2026 premium: anomaly detection over your AI traffic

Static thresholds still lie for AI even harder than for CPUs (LLM latency is *wildly* non-normal; a "safe" 5-second threshold is either a scream factory or a lullaby). So the mature approach is the same one Google-style operations converged on: **anomaly detection tuned on *your* usage patterns** (TimesFM-style forecasters, or your observability vendor's equivalent), triggering on "unusual-for-you" shifts in latency, tokens, failure, or interval. Plus **non-telemetry signals** the agents can fold in — e.g., customer feedback tied to a SLO-violation spike. Telemetry says "different"; agents + humans say "dangerous" (the Chapter 11 ceremony, all over again).

## The trace that makes it tweetable — what a *good* AI trace shows

The "dossier," not the scream, applied to a model call inside a user request:

```
GET /assistant → trace
  span: orchestration        (agent picked tool plan)
  span: context-assembly     (17 chunks retrieved, recall 0.83, fresh 2h)
  span: llm-call             model=claude-x prompt_v=v42 ctx_fill=61%
                            tokens_in=3.2k tokens_out=1.1k finish=max_tokens ⚠
  span: toolcall             search_billing ∫ ok  (result hash a91f…)
  span: reply-write          db txn ok  (idempotency key reused ✓)
  total: 8.4s (p99 of cohort = 2.1s)  cost $0.041
```

Reads like a novel's cast list, and every element is *actionable*: the max-tokens ⚠, the context fill, the recall, the cost. That's the standard to hold your stack to — and to teach your AI SRE agents to *produce*.

## Beginner's three moves (any stack, this month)

1. **Instrument first.** Turn on OTel GenAI spans for any LLM use in your org (if "turn on" isn't possible, that's the immediate procurement conversation, because the vendor isn't doing observability).
2. **The token dashboard.** Cheapest highest-value chart you'll ever build: tokens/task per agent, plus cost, at a daily grain, with a 7-day trend line.
3. **One postmortem on a behavior-behavior mystery.** The next time "the model was weird," pin the version, replay the trace, count the tokens, quote the finish-reason. One such autopsy will sell the whole observability-of-AI idea to your team better than this chapter ever could.
