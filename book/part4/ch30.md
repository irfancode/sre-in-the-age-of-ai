# Chapter 30 — The Crisis Playbook: Escalate, Contain, and Don't Panic

The original chapter's greatest gift to humanity is simple and eternal: **the human response to an emergency is not the thing to be clever about.** It should be *boring, rehearsed, and predictable* — a liturgy, not an improvisation. When the building is on fire, you want the fire department's boring, rehearsed drill, not a brilliant improviser. We keep exactly that liturgy; we now choreograph ninja-scribes and AI first-responders into it.

## The permanent incident liturgy (unchanged in spirit, sharpened in detail)

### 1. The five phases (with reading-time)

1. **Recognize** — *something is wrong.* (Open the dossier; Chapter 25: detection with an AI gatherer.)
2. **Respond / recover** — *protect users first.* (Mitigation, minimize further harm, restore service.)
3. **Investigate** — *understand while you're stabilized.* (Hypothesis-led, evidence-based, receipts everywhere.)
4. **Repair** — *fix the underlying cause.* (Chapter 29 closes the loop.)
5. **Review** — *learn and improve.* (Postmortem culture, Chapter 28.)

### 2. The permanent roles

Three roles, assigned *before* the fire (not during it, and with a deputy for each so there's no "who's the commander?" debate at 2 a.m.):

- **Incident Commander (IC)** — the *coordinator*, not the hero: owns the *process* (who does what by when), not the tech. The IC's job is to make sure all the other roles *get what they need* and that decisions are informed. **The dog that keeps the sheep together, not the wolf that brings down the problem.**
- **Operations Lead (OL)** — the deep-technical hands: runs the search/debug/mitigation, driven by the chapter's evidence discipline (Chapter 29). Uses the ai-scribe + AI first-responder as force multipliers.
- **Communications Lead (CL)** — the voice to the outside: users, product, execs, regulators seizure, and *augmented* by an agent drafting updates at the pace the audience needs (write the note, human edits + approves, ship).
- **PLUS (2016 → 2026): the AI scribe.** A dedicated agent whose entire job is *the timeline*: every action, claim, hypothesis, and smelly rumor — stamped, attributed, and searchable, *as it happens*. The classic chapter pleaded for this discipline in runbooks (minutes are gold); in 2026 the scribe is *in the room, permanently, at the speed of the events themselves.* The postmortem (and the ledger, and the replay suite) become cheap because the timeline already exists — and it's *timeline-with-receipts*, not Vibes-of-Remembering.

### 3. The five red lights (escalation conditions — the rope to pull)

| Condition | Escalate when… | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| **SLA/SLO breach** | — any error-budget drawdown (your SLOs, Chapter 15) breaks or is about to break | Major incident page, IC + on-call + duty manager |
| **High-ticket meltdown** | — sustained >100 tickets/hr (or a critical class) | Round-robin team + AI triage agent |
| **Deadline approaching** | — launch or external promise is at risk (Chapters 16, 20, 21) | That team's DM + IC |
| **Permanent damage** | — data loss or any irreversible action happened (Chapter 27's "frozen demand") | Security + legal + IC, *immediately* |
| **Repeated detection failure** | — "this exact failure already happened and our monitoring missed it (Chapters 25, 31)" | The monitoring-owners + the agent-safety team (Chapter 28's "would the agent have caught it?" — if not, why not?) |

### 4. The frozen list (things you DO NOT do during the emergency)

- **No blame. At all.** (Postmortem is later, and blameless — Chapter 28.)
- **No fix-attempts that deepen the fire.** (Change one thing at a time, evidence-led — Chapter 29.)
- **No "the AI did it" hand-waving.** The crew is accountable as a crew; machines don't own, humans must (Chapters 22, 28).

### 5. The launch-safety brakes (because AI-era launches are faster and scarier)

- **The cascade warning:** in 2026, incidents are *compound* (Chapter 26): "main database slows → agents thrash → API dependency falls over → coin flip cascade." Rehearse the *cross-stack* blast radius on the autonomy table (Chapters 27, 13, 22).
- **The incident dashboard:** a live, shared, single-pane-of-glass "incident state document" (classic Appendix C → our [Appendix D](appendix/d/)) that tracks: what's broken, who's working it, what's next, what's been tried, what the receipts are.

## In one sentence for the max-velocity era

> **"Emergency response is a craft of containment while the fire is lit — mitigate first with evidence, stop the bleeding before the diagnosis, and make every minute count toward the timeline, not the blame."**

That trailed off, it keeps the crew from Olympic trap again. Let me finish the thought: *mitigate first, contain, and only then* **— and never let speed outrun your receipts: every assertion you make in an incident belongs on a timeline with a stamp.**
