# Chapter 23 — Reliability for AI Systems: MLOps & LLMOps, Without Tears

By now you've internalized the discipline's metaphysics: *reliability = keeping promises*. The only new thing in this chapter is *what promises an AI system makes* — and they're sneakier than a web service's, because the A in "AI" added a very noisy new variable.

## The landscape in one map

Your AI product is really three layers of promises, and 2026 engineers have given each a name:

1. **MLOps** — making *model pipelines* (training, retraining, drift detection, deployment) reliable. Think "DevOps for machine learning."
2. **LLMOps** — making *LLM-based features* reliable: prompts, context, evals, tokens, latency, cost, and the *quality* of generation.
3. **Agent operations** — making *autonomous actors* reliable: scope, safety SLIs, loop control, memory hygiene (Chapters 24 and 27).

Your job is not to become a data scientist. It's to bring the SRE toolkit to a stack that behaves like software *with a personality*.

## The elephant: why AI reliability feels different

A web server's contract is boring: "2xx, under 200ms, as fast as the disk." An LLM's contract is squishy:

- **Non-determinism.** Same input, slightly different output, *every single time*. "Reproducible behavior" needs seeds, temperature=0 discipline, and *still* isn't guaranteed at the API level.
- **Graceful-sounding failure.** Models fail *confidently*: no exception, no 500, just a wrong or nonsensical answer with perfect composure. This is the architectural reason the industry now says *"reliability for AI = availability + latency + **judgment**"* (Chapter 24's topic), and it's why your *golden signals alone* can't save you.
- **Long, expensive tails.** A slow prompt can buffer your p95 for minutes and cost you real money per hour of foolishness.
- **Ecosystem fragility.** Model providers change behavior, deprecate APIs, and throttle you without emailing *you*; embeddings drift; RAG indexes go stale. Your deployment is now coupled to a moving, partially-proprietary brain.

None of it is an argument *against* AI. It's an argument for treating AI as a **first-class system with SLOs, budgets, gates, and fallbacks** rather than as a magic function.

## The reliability toolkit, ported faithfully

### Evals: the new "unit tests" (the thing you must build before anything else)

You cannot test a model like code — there's no expected output for "summarize this email." So you build the AI-era test rig:

- **Golden sets** — curated input→expected-behavior examples ("the model must refuse this; the agent must call tool X here; this summary must mention the refund and the deadline"). **Version the goldens** like code — an unaudited eval-set is a test suite you can't trust.
- **Automated evals** — plus *model-as-judge* scoring tiers ("LLM judges an LLM") for prose quality, with real humans *sampling* the judges (llm-judge pipelines are fast and cheap; their verdicts need spot-audits, because a judge model can hallucinate approval).
- **The regression rule:** any prompt/model/context change must *replay* the goldens and the *incident library* (this quarter's worst hits — the famous `replay suite` from Chapter 16). If the new model can't handle last quarter's data poison pattern, it doesn't promote.
- **The honest caveat:** eval ≠ proof. "92% on our goldens" is evidence, not a guarantee; production is the final exam.

### Prompt & config as code (the release pipeline, ported)

Everything from Chapter 9 applies — *prompts are software*:

- Prompts, system messages, model versions, temperatures, context policies: **version-controlled, reviewed, canaried** like any change.
- **Prompt/model swaps = releases.** Same progressive rollout: canary a slice of traffic → watch *quality SLOs* (not just latency) → widen → full, with a rollback that's a config flip, not a resurrection ritual.
- **Drift and decay are the enemy of settled.** Model providers sneakily improve (or regress); your golden suite drifts out of date. *Re-run evals on a schedule* and alert on quality trend-lines, not just soup.

### Cost & latency as reliability signals (this feels exotic; let it)

For an LLM stack, **tokens are the load unit** — and therefore the reliability metric:

- *Tokens per request / per task* rising week over week is a **leading indicator** (typically surfacing 24–48h before visible output decay). E.g., context bloat makes each call slower, pricier, and *more error-prone*. Watch it like queue depth.
- *Token budget* per agent/task = a hard guardrail (Chapter 25 dials this in).
- *p95 latency on the model path* — with a **fallback path designed when the provider sneezes** (a cheaper local model for the easy 80%; a queue-the-and-reply-later path; a deterministic answer for the 10% that must never hang). Whether your AI tier is served by a paid API, self-hosted GPUs, or your cloud's service — **single-provider is a single point of failure**, on the reliability map marked like any other SPOF.

### The data-stack reliability you can't skip

- **RAG with an SLA on freshness** — a stale index serves yesterday's truth confidently (Chapter 26's integrity for the AI stack); refresh on schedule, version the batches, reconcile the counts.
- **Embeddings & fine-tunes as state** — versioned, hashable, rollbackable (Chapter 19).
- **Retrieval quality alarms** — a *low-retrieval-recall* alert (the model can't find the facts it's supposed to) is your early-warning that answers are about to drown in hallucination.

## The four-lived lifecycle of a model in production (your SRE map)

1. **Build** — eval suites, goldens, versioned prompts.
2. **Gate** — canary with quality-monitoring, admit/rollback on regression.
3. **Run** — monitor quality/latency/cost/drift; refresh RAG; watch tokens; chaos the provider (Chapter 26).
4. **Retire** — sunset the model/prompt calmly (a preference change shouldn't be a festival of broken features); with an *eval-replay* to prove the successor is at least as good.

## What the beginner should actually build first (ranked)

1. **Eval-0:** 20–50 golden examples for your org's most important AI use case and a button to run them. (Highest leverage; so cheap it's almost criminal.)
2. **A latency + token dashboard** for any model in the critical path, at p95, with a *provider-down* runbook (who to call, which cutover, what the degraded experience is).
3. **One entry in the log ledger for an AI incident** — even if it's "the model changed something without paging," that's your first AI postmortem, and it's *precious* (Chapter 15).

## The one-line creed for AI reliability

> **Models are not magic functions; they are high-variance systems with providers, memories, and opinions. Treat them like it: evals for truth, budgets for tokens, fallbacks for outages, gates for changes, and postmortems for their confident lies.**
