# Chapter 17 — Software Engineering in SRE: Build, Don't Burrow

The original book noticed that SRE teams, left unmanaged, would happily spend their entire existence building the same categories of internal tools. It named the playbook: **if SRE repeats a task or a question more than twice, the answer is software.** The tooling engineering is not the optional icing; it's the *product* SRE ships. The 50% cap (Chapter 1) exists precisely so there's always room in the week for it.

## The classic "SRE product catalog" (still yours to build)

- **Self-service provisioning** — developers stand up dev/staging environments without an SRE ticket.
- **Config management as code** — changes reviewable, rollbackable, auditable (Terraform, Ansible, helm, etc.).
- **Binary/artifact release pipeline** — the Canary-Ramp-Drain machinery of Chapter 9.
- **Monitoring + runbook + on-call glue** — alerts that link to their own instructions.
- **The "เนมละ" observability layer** — tracing, dashboards, anomaly detection.
- **Playbook & knowledge systems** — the tribal knowledge made queryable.

In 2026 almost all of these have become *platforms* you integrate and *agents* you delegate to, rather than bespoke builds. The engineering you personally write is the part that *expresses your org's specific physics* — and, increasingly, the part that *orchestrates the AI*.

## The 2026 shape of SRE software engineering

Here's the updated product catalog for an AI-era team — and notice how the *human, machine, agent* division of labor from Chapter 3 shows up in every row:

| Product (2026) | It does | Division |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy/approval engine** | Which agent may do what, gated by safety ledger + budgets | Agents propose; engine enforces; humans set policy |
| **Eval & replay harness** | Model/prompt regression gates; replay last quarter's incidents vs. today's stack | Machines run evals; humans design the goldens |
| **AI first-responder + dossier** | Triage, enrichment, RCA-with-receipts, escalation | Agents investigate; humans adjudicate |
| **Playbook/insight knowledge system** | RAG over past incidents so agents & humans share a perfect memory | Machines index; humans curate the important bits |
| **Observability for AI** | OTel GenAI spans, token/context budgets, behavior SLIs | Machines emit; agents read; humans set intent |
| **Guardrail & policy-as-code** | Safety SLIs, policy gates, drift/regen checks on prompts & memory | Humans write policy; machines+agents obey |

## The eternal laws of SRE software engineering (updated)

1. **The tools exist to give time back to the team.** Each build must be explainable as "this kills X hours/month of toil" — or it's a hobby castle. (In the AI era, add: "…or this *verifies* better than the 40-manual-review it replaces" — the verification-tax cure.)
2. **Design for the person who will take over.** 2026 version: *design for the agent that will take over.* Code, configs, prompts, and runbooks are read by machines now. If your runbook can't be executed by an intern-or-agent from start to finish, it's a diary, not a document.
3. **Small, boring, and composable beats clever.** The "boring tech" law from Chapter 10 is doubled for anything an agent must reason about.
4. **The 50% rule is a mandate, not folklore.** A team that burrows into an endless custom dashboard while 60% of its week is tickets is a support org wearing an SRE name tag. Rebalance, redirect ops load to dev, or shrink scope — then build.

## Two 2026 warnings before you sprint off

**Warning 1 — Don't build what you can rent... to the point of sameness.** Platform fatigue is real. But the AI-era inverse trap is rarer and worse: building bespoke "AI agents" for every toil (each needing its own guardrails, evals, and upkeep). Standardize: one scaffold, one eval harness, one governance layer — *then* point point, don't clone.

**Warning 2 — Your own tools need on-call too.** An agent-drafted automation suite without owners, budgets, or rollbacks is a liability you wrote to feel productive. The tools have SLOs, error budgets, and audit trails — the same discipline you demand of product. Especially the AI ones: *unfunded automation is debt* (Chapter 8). Sunset what outlives its maintenance.

## Beginner's first two builds (choose your adventure)

- **The "recent-changes trail."** Tomorrow: a page that, given any service, shows the last 10 changes next to the last 10 alert flinches. This single tool pays for itself in headaches on night one. (Machine-grade simple; agent-grade if you add correlation.)
- **The EVAL-0 for your team's lurking LLM use.** If your org already uses AI somewhere, draft the first 20 golden examples of "this is what good output looks like" for that use case. You've just built the seed of an eval harness — the highest-leverage thing a novice SRE can construct.

Build, don't burrow. Ship the system that ends the tickets. (And yes, your AI can now draft whole swaths of it. Your job is the design, the guardrails, and the verification.)
