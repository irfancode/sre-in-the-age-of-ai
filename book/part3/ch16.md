# Chapter 16 — Testing for Reliability & Tracking Outages

Two classic chapters, bundled because they're two halves of the same brain: *measure what you've suffered* (tracking) and *engineer what you'll survive* (testing). In 2026 they both gain an AI dimension, and neither loses an ounce of importance.

## Part one: Tracking outages (you can't manage what you don't count)

Every outage is data. A *broken* org lets it evaporate into anecdote; a *reliable* org compels it into a ledger. The ledger deserves five columns:

- **Detection time** — from first user pain to first alarm.
- **Response time** — from alarm to a human/agent engaged.
- **Mitigation time** — from engaged to users relieved (this is the MTTR you actually feel).
- **Resolution time** — the underlying cause fixed (the MTTR your architecture feels).
- **Error budget consumed** — how much of the quarter's allowed failure this story ate (Chapter 4 — the number that gives outages their *business weight*).

Now the offender: each entry is also tagged *recurrence* (same-flavor failure as x months ago? → your preventer pipeline is leaking), *detection-path* (monitoring? user complaint? agent? → bought visibility), and *type* (deploy, config, dependency, AI-behavior, human).

**The five-number rhythm:** leaders don't read incident stories for breakfast; they read *quarterly trends*: MTTR trend, detection-time trend, budget-burn-by-type, recurrence rate, and top-3 incident flavors. If those five get reported with the same reverence as revenue, your company has internalized SRE.

**2026 additions:** your agent's live-incident timelines make the ledger *almost* free (auto-stamped times, auto-classified types, auto-recurrence matching against the postmortem library). The human's job is the second layer the machine can't do: *is this really the same flavor, or a shapeshifter?* And AI-behavior incidents get their own ledger (prompt regressions, model-drift events, agent oversteps) with the judgment-SLI intake from [Chapter 24](part4/ch24/).

## Part two: Testing for reliability — the graduated test ladder

The classic book's gift here was the idea of a **test ladder**: each rung answers "will this change hurt?" with increasing fidelity and cost:

1. **Unit tests** — the snake in its own terrarium: a function, its inputs, its expected outputs. Fast, cheap, local. (2026: also *prompt*lint and *schema* checks.)
2. **Integration tests** — territory with neighbors: does service A actually talk to service B, and does the DB actually behave when they do?
3. **System / end-to-end tests** — the full zoo: real environments, real config, real dependencies. The famous "Shakespeare on multiple Borg clusters" test belongs here.
4. **Canaries & SLO-gated rollouts** — the *user-aware* rung (Chapter 9): real traffic, small slice, health gates.
5. **Chaos / fault injection / Game Days** — deliberately breaking things to discover the seatbelts (Chapter 26 expands this for agents).

Rule of the ladder: **spend each ounce of confidence where the cost of being wrong is highest.** A cheap unit test that fails early is worth a golden dashboard that fails late.

### The 2026 additions to the ladder

- **Evals (the crucial new rung for AI).** A *model* or *prompt* isn't tested like code — there's no "expected output" for prose. **Evals** are golden datasets with expected behaviors ("given this ticket, summarize accurately"; "given this policy question, refuse or accept?"); every prompt/model change runs evals, and regressions block. If your AI product has no eval harness, its "tests" are vibes with a veneer. (Full treatment in Chapter 23.)
- **Agent capability gate:** before expanding what an agent *may* do, test at larger scope progressively: sandbox → shadow → canary → fleet, with live safety SLIs gating each promotion and auto-rollback on policy flinch (the "SLO gate" pattern).
- **Regression guards for the machines' memory:** your AI systems accumulate context, memories, fine-tunes. Everything that *learns* drifts. The reliability answer: **replay** — run last quarter's hard incidents against your current model/agents and ask "would we still survive?" That's the AI-age version of a regression suite, and it's beautiful.
- **Property & metamorphic testing** (bonus): for chaos-resistant confidence, test *invariants* ("sum of balances is conserved", "every write has a read"): even when AI changes the details, the laws stay testable.

## The testing culture that both chapters were really about

Neither tracking nor testing works without the unglamorous part: **tests as a habit, and numbers as a language, rather than a checklist to be gamed.** Signs you've made it: developers write tests because they hurt when absent; leaders ask "what did we learn and what did we break?" at least as often as "what did we ship?"; and no change — code, config, *prompt*, *model*, *agent-scope* — promotes without its gates passing.

Beginner note: you don't need a company to practice. Take a personal project, write the outage ledger for its last three real incidents, draft *one* test per subsystem, and invent one Game Day ("what if this API key expires on Friday?"). Congratulations — you're practicing the discipline, and the patterns transfer everywhere.
