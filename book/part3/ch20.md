# Chapter 20 — Pipelines & Data Integrity

Two classic chapters, again happily married: the machinery that *moves and transforms data* (pipelines) and the promise that *what you read is what you wrote* (integrity). The second is an eternal law; the first is a shape-shifter that by 2026 has learned to chew tokens and fine-tunes.

## Part one: Pipelines — the factory line for data

A **pipe** is a chain of stages: *extract → transform → load*, or *collect → clean → feature → train → evaluate → serve*. Because the stages are numerous and the data enormous, pipelines earn their own reliability discipline, with three rules that survive every era:

1. **Exactly-once is a lie, at-least-once is the promise, idempotency is the cure.** Messages will be redelivered, jobs will be re-run. Design every stage so "run it twice" does the same damage as "run it once": dedupe keys, upsert semantics, "if completed, skip." The 2026 corollary is cruel: **AI is busy writing pipelines where "twice" can differ**, because LLM stages aren't deterministic. Every *agent-in-a-pipe* stage must be run as "generate + verify (re-run with a seed, cross-check, fallback to deterministic)" or your "idempotent" pipeline becomes a small lottery.
2. **Watch the boundaries.** The hardest failures live in handoffs: schema changes, dead-letter sinks, transient sources. Version your schemas; never silently drop records (count them out loud: *records-in = records-out + rejects + dropped*, audited).
3. **Freshness & backpressure are the vitals.** Data gets old: late-arriving records, stalled stages, growing queues. Monitor *age* ("the newest record is 47 minutes overdue") not just volume — the oldest SRE trick nobody skips because it always works.

### The 2026 pipeline additions

- **GPU/ML pipelines are now a first-class citizen:** training runs, evaluation sweeps, embedding refreshes, and RAG indexing eat dedicated, expensive hardware and have distinctive failure modes (queue-hopping jobs, GPU OOMs, checkpoint corruption). Give them the same courtesy as any business pipeline: checkpoints (resumable), gates, and ops owners.
- **Token/LLM stages in production flows:** summarization-in-the-middle, classification, extraction. Each becomes a reliability surface: *latency variance* (an LLM stage can fluff the p95), *cost drift* (Chapter 25's token budget), and *output quality* (a cheap eval on a slice of the stream). If an LLM is in the critical path, it has an SLO and a fallback path when the provider sneezes.
- **Agents as pipeline operators:** an agent that watches the pipeline, raises the dead-letter rate, and retries the stalled stage is a fantastic cron-sitter (Chapter 19). Keep the agent's *observations* agentic and its *actions* guarded.

## Part two: Data integrity — the promise that never ages

The crown jewel of the classic chapters: **"What you read is what you wrote"** — and, for durability, "what you wrote is still there next year." Every regulation, every bank, every customer contract that ever lived exists because someone decided this promise is non-negotiable. The integrity toolkit, still perfection:

- **Checksums & cryptographic hashes at rest and in motion** — corruption is detected, not assumed absent "because it says so."
- **Replication + periodic restore drills** — yes, test the *restore*, not just the backup. A backup nobody has ever restored is a rumor.
- **Detecting silent bit-rot** — periodic re-read/verify of the archive; storage does decay, that's why checksums exist.
- **The daily reconciliation** — "what we think we stored" vs. "what the storage system says" counted, out loud, every day.
- **Write-path discipline** — writes acknowledged only after durable; transactional semantics for anything money-adjacent.

**The 2026 integrity additions (predictable by now):**
- **Model/agent "data" integrity:** your *fine-tune*, your *RAG index*, your *prompt store*, and your *memory* are user-impacting data. Corrupt a RAG index (a bad embedding batch) and your AI confidently serves garbage — with perfect uptime. Integritify the AI stack: hash and version weights/prompts/indexes; verify the evals you claim (a changed eval set invalidates your "pass"); and *replay* the critical paths after any data change (Chapter 16's replay suite).
- **The supply-chain integrity chapter is Chapter 29** — but the one-line verdict belongs here: if you can't prove *where your model's training data and your agent's tools came from*, you can't prove the outputs they promise. Integrity starts upstream.

## The pipeline & integrity scorecard

```
PL_IN  − swept under the rug?      no, counted
PL_OUT − schema changes versioned? yes
PL_AGE − freshness alarmed?        yes (not just volume)
PL_2X  − reruns idempotent?        yes (AI stages: generate+verify)
PL_LLM − token stages have SLO + fallback? yes
INT_CC − checksums everywhere?     yes (incl. AI weights/prompts/indexes)
INT_BK − restore tested recently?  yes (drill, not just backup)
INT_RE − daily reconciliation?     yes
INT_AI − evals/replay honest + versioned? yes
```

## The two paragraphs to carry out of this chapter

**On pipelines:** treat every handoff as a potential verse of poetry*; count what goes in and out (out loud); make reruns harmless; give any AI stage a fallback by default.

**On integrity:** the only data you can promise is the data you can *verify*. Checksums, restores, reconciliations — and in 2026, hashes and versioning for your *models, prompts, indexes, and memories*. A system that trusts its own storage without checking is a system that will one day read its own fiction and call it fact.
